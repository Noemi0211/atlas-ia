## The AI ecosystem is enormous

New AI tools appear every week. It can be overwhelming. But the good news is that **they can be grouped into clear categories**. Once you understand the categories, everything starts to make sense.

## The 7 main categories

### 1. Conversational Assistants 💬
Chatbots that answer questions and help with a wide range of tasks.

**Examples**: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini

They are the most common entry point to AI. You can write to them in natural language and get useful answers.

### 2. Coding Tools 💻
AI specialised in helping you program.

**Examples**: GitHub Copilot, Cursor

If you program, these tools can multiply your productivity.

### 3. Image Generation 🎨
They create images from text descriptions.

**Examples**: DALL-E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion

From illustrations to photorealistic photographs.

### 4. Audio and Video 🎵
AI for creating, editing or transforming multimedia content.

**Examples**: ElevenLabs, Runway

Narration, music, video editing and more.

### 5. No-Code Platforms 🧩
They let you create automations and apps without coding.

**Examples**: Make, Zapier

Automate complex workflows with visual interfaces.

### 6. Productivity ⚡
Tools to work faster and organise yourself better.

**Examples**: Notion AI, Perplexity

Summaries, intelligent search and knowledge management.

### 7. Research 🔬
Academic and advanced search tools.

**Examples**: Perplexity, Elicit

Search with verified sources and analysis of academic papers.

## Why do categories matter?

Understanding the categories helps you:

- **Know what to look for**: If you need to create images, do not waste time with chatbots
- **Compare apples with apples**: It makes no sense to compare ChatGPT with Midjourney
- **Discover tools**: If you like a category, explore the other options
- **Understand the market**: Each category has its leaders and its niches

## The reality: you will need several tools

There is no single tool that does everything perfectly. Professionals usually use **3-5 different tools** depending on the task.

### Practical example:
- **Quick questions**: ChatGPT
- **Document analysis**: Claude
- **Search with sources**: Perplexity
- **Create images**: Midjourney or DALL-E
- **Programming**: Cursor or Copilot

<Callout type="tip">
Do not try to learn all the tools at once. Pick one category, master one tool, and then gradually expand.
</Callout>

## The ecosystem changes fast

What is the leader today may be surpassed tomorrow. The important thing is not to memorise the current tools, but to understand **how to evaluate them**:

1. What task do I need to do?
2. What are the options in that category?
3. Which one best fits my budget and skill level?
4. Which one offers the best quality for MY use case?
