## Beyond text

So far, most of the AI models we have seen work mainly with text. But the real world is multimodal: we combine images, sounds, words and gestures to communicate. Multimodal AI seeks to replicate this capability.

<Callout type="info">
A multimodal model is one that can process and generate information in multiple formats: text, images, audio and video, all within a single system.
</Callout>

## What does multimodal mean?

The term **multimodal** refers to the ability to work with multiple modalities or data formats. While a traditional language model only understands text, a multimodal model can:

- Read and describe images
- Transcribe and generate audio
- Analyze and create video
- Combine all of the above

## Main multimodal models

### GPT-4V / GPT-4o

OpenAI integrated vision into GPT-4, making it possible to analyze images, diagrams and scanned documents. GPT-4o added real-time audio capabilities.

### Claude 3 / 4 (Sonnet)

Anthropic incorporated vision with advanced capabilities for analyzing images, charts and complex documents.

### Gemini

Google's model was born multimodal: text, images, audio and video natively. It is designed to reason across multiple formats simultaneously.

### Llama 3.2 (Meta)

Meta's most recent version added open-source multimodal capabilities, democratizing access to this technology.

## How multimodality works

### Unified architecture

Modern multimodal models use an architecture that converts all modalities into a common representation space. Text is tokenized, images are divided into patches, audio is converted into spectrograms, and everything is processed with a single transformer.

### Specialized encoders

Each modality has an encoder that extracts relevant features:

- **Vision:** CNNs or ViTs (Vision Transformers) to process pixels
- **Audio:** Wave2Vec or Whisper to convert sound into representations
- **Text:** traditional language embeddings

### Multimodal fusion

The key step is combining information from different sources. There are three main approaches:

1. **Early fusion:** fuse before processing
2. **Late fusion:** process separately and combine at the end
3. **Hybrid fusion:** mix at multiple stages

<Callout type="tip">
Early fusion is more powerful because it allows the model to learn relationships between modalities, but it is computationally more expensive.
</Callout>

## Cross-cutting applications

Multimodality opens up possibilities that go beyond the sum of its parts:

- **Multimodal search:** find images by describing them with text
- **Universal assistants:** that see, hear and speak
- **Document analysis:** extract information from invoices, reports and charts
- **Visual translation:** describe an image in another language
