## Block summary

In this block we have explored how artificial intelligence can transform teaching in all its dimensions:

### Lesson 1 — Fundamentals
AI personalises learning, automates administrative tasks and enables the analysis of educational data. 21st-century teachers need to develop digital skills to integrate it with judgment.

### Lesson 2 — Tools
There is a tool for every need: versatile assistants (ChatGPT, Claude), educational platforms (Khanmigo, Quizlet), content creators (Canva, Gamma) and assessment systems (Gradescope).

### Lesson 3 — Curriculum planning
AI speeds up the design of teaching units, generates learning objectives and facilitates curriculum adaptations. A well-structured prompt is the key to getting useful results.

### Lesson 4 — Content creation
Explanatory texts, exercises, images and presentations can be generated in minutes. Critical review by the teacher remains essential before they reach the classroom.

### Lesson 5 — Assessment and feedback
Rubrics, automated grading and personalised feedback are possible with AI. Combining automated assessment with human judgment offers the best results.

### Lesson 6 — Ethics and challenges
Privacy, algorithmic bias, the digital divide and the new role of teachers are challenges we must address actively and reflectively.

## Final self-assessment

1. What are the three areas of impact of AI in education?
2. Name at least four AI tools for the classroom and their main function
3. What elements should a prompt include to plan a teaching unit?
4. Why is it important to critically review AI-generated content?
5. How can AI help with formative assessment?
6. What three ethical challenges does the use of AI in education pose?
7. What do you think the role of teachers will be in the coming years?

## Block glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---------|------------|
| **Intelligent tutor** | System that guides students in a personalised way |
| **Prompt** | Instruction given to an AI model to generate a response |
| **Algorithmic bias** | Systematic prejudices reflected in the decisions of an algorithm |
| **GDPR** | General Data Protection Regulation (European regulation) |
| **Digital divide** | Inequality in access to and use of digital technologies |
| **Formative feedback** | Feedback that helps students improve during the learning process |

## Additional resources

### Recommended reading

- *Artificial Intelligence in Education* — UNESCO (free online)
- *Teaching with AI* — OpenAI's practical guide for teachers
- *AI and education: a guide for teachers* — INTEF (Instituto Nacional de Tecnologías Educativas)

### Communities

- **EduTwitter** and **EduMastodon**: follow teachers who share their AI experiences
- **Red de Docentes Innovadores**: groups on LinkedIn and Telegram
- **INTEF forums**: courses and communities of practice

### Tools to explore

- **ChatGPT** (chat.openai.com) — Versatile assistant
- **Claude** (claude.ai) — Alternative with great analytical capacity
- **Canva Magic Studio** (canva.com) — Visual creation
- **Gamma** (gamma.app) — Presentations with AI
- **Perplexity** (perplexity.ai) — Assisted research
