## Augmented assessment

Assessment is one of the most complex and laborious tasks for teachers. AI can speed up the process without losing depth, offering rich, personalised feedback that would be impossible to generate manually at scale.

<Callout type="info">
AI does not assess for you: it helps you assess better. The final decision, the interpretation of results and supporting students remain your responsibility.
</Callout>

## Generating rubrics

Rubrics are essential for objective assessment, but designing them takes time. AI can generate them in seconds:

> "Create a rubric to assess an argumentative essay on climate change for 1st year of Bachillerato (post-16 education). Include 4 criteria: structure, argumentation, use of sources and expository clarity. For each criterion, define 4 achievement levels."

### Advantages of AI-generated rubrics

- **Consistency:** all rubrics follow the same format
- **Speed:** you generate rubrics for any task in minutes
- **Personalisation:** you adapt criteria and levels according to your context

## Automated grading

### What AI can grade

- Multiple-choice and true/false exercises
- Short open-answer questions
- Essays and compositions (with supervision)
- Maths problems with working out
- Grammar and vocabulary exercises

### How to use it for grading

> "You are a history teacher. Grade the following answer from a 4th year of ESO student about the causes of the First World War. Point out strengths, errors and areas for improvement. End with a concrete piece of advice for improvement."

<Callout type="tip">
Use AI for a first pass at grading and then review the most subjective aspects yourself. This workflow reduces your workload without losing quality.
</Callout>

## Personalised feedback

### Feedback at scale

AI can generate individualised comments for each student, something that is impossible to do manually in large groups:

> "I have graded 25 maths exams on equations. For each student, generate a 3-line comment that mentions: (1) what they did well, (2) what they need to improve and (3) a recommended exercise to practise."

### Types of feedback

| Type | Description | When to use it |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| Immediate | Correction on the spot | Formative exercises |
| Descriptive | Explains the error and how to improve it | Written tasks |
| Comparative | Shows progress relative to previous work | Continuous assessment |
| Motivational | Reinforces achievements and encourages to keep going | All stages |

## Detecting early difficulties

AI can analyse patterns in students' responses to identify:

- **Misunderstood concepts** that recur in the group
- **Students at risk** before their grades drop
- **Learning styles** predominant in the classroom
- **Knowledge gaps** between different groups

<Callout type="warning">
Early detection is a tool for intervening, not for labelling. Always use it with sensitivity and in combination with your direct observation of students.
</Callout>

## Limitations of AI-based assessment

1. **Limited understanding:** AI does not understand the students' real context
2. **Algorithmic bias:** it may penalise certain writing styles
3. **False positives/negatives:** especially in originality detection
4. **Technological dependency:** it does not work without an internet connection

## Good practices

- **Combine** automatic assessment with human assessment
- **Tell** students that you use AI as support
- **Review** the grades before returning them
- **Update** your prompts based on the results you get
- **Keep** pedagogical judgement as the final criterion
