## Ethical responsibility in the digital classroom

Integrating AI into education is not just a technical matter: it is a pedagogical and ethical decision. Every tool we bring into the classroom raises questions about privacy, equity, autonomy and the role of teachers.

<Callout type="info">
Technology is never neutral. Every algorithm embeds human decisions that can reflect biases, privilege certain perspectives and affect different groups of students unevenly.
</Callout>

## Privacy and data protection

### Student data

When we use AI tools, students' data becomes exposed:

- **Personal information:** names, emails, ages
- **Academic data:** performance, difficulties, learning styles
- **Behavioural patterns:** schedules, preferences, interactions

### Key questions

- Where is this data stored?
- Who has access to it?
- How long is it kept?
- Is it used to train models?
- Does the tool comply with the GDPR (or local regulations)?

<Callout type="warning">
Never enter students' personal data into free AI tools without checking their privacy policy. Many platforms use the data entered to train their models.
</Callout>

## Algorithmic bias

AI models learn from existing data, which may contain historical or cultural biases:

### Examples in education

- **Linguistic bias:** penalising dialect varieties or colloquial expressions
- **Cultural bias:** examples that reflect a dominant culture
- **Gender bias:** associating certain professions or skills with a gender
- **Socio-economic bias:** assumptions about access to technology or resources

### How to mitigate it

1. **Diversify** your prompts and sources of information
2. **Review** the generated content from multiple perspectives
3. **Complement** AI with diverse, inclusive materials
4. **Educate** students about the existence of algorithmic bias

## Digital divide and equity

Not all students have the same access to technology:

- **Connectivity:** not all families have internet at home
- **Devices:** low-end computers, tablets and mobile phones
- **Digital skills:** very variable levels among students
- **Family support:** some families can help, others cannot

### What to do

- **Assess** your students' real access before planning
- **Offer** offline alternatives whenever possible
- **Design** activities that do not depend exclusively on AI
- **Request** resources from the school to reduce the gap

| Challenge | Risk | Mitigation |
|---------|--------|------------|
| Privacy | Data exposure | Verify policies, anonymise |
| Bias | Discrimination | Review and diversify |
| Digital divide | Inequality | Offline alternatives |
| Dependence | Loss of autonomy | Critical and reflective use |

## The role of teachers in the AI era

Far from disappearing, the role of teachers becomes more important:

1. **Critical guide:** teaches how to use AI with judgment
2. **Content curator:** selects and adapts what AI generates
3. **Emotional companion:** AI cannot offer emotional support
4. **Promoter of values:** ethics, empathy, critical thinking
5. **Experience designer:** creates meaningful learning situations

<Callout type="tip">
The classroom of the future is not a classroom without teachers: it is a classroom where teachers have more time for what really matters: connecting, inspiring and guiding.
</Callout>
