## There are proven techniques that work

You do not have to invent prompts from scratch every time. There are proven techniques you can learn and apply depending on what you need.

## Zero-shot prompting

It is the most basic technique: **you ask the AI to do something without giving it examples**.

**Example:**
```
Classify this text as "positive" or "negative":
"The movie was incredible, the best of the year."
```

The AI knows what "positive" and "negative" mean without you giving it examples.

**Works well for:** Simple, well-defined tasks.

**Does not work well for:** Complex tasks or tasks with very specific formats.

## Few-shot prompting

You give the AI **examples** of what you expect before asking it to do the real task.

**Example:**
```
Translate these sentences into formal English:

Spanish: "Hola, ¿cómo estás?"
English: "Hello, how are you?"

Spanish: "Muchas gracias por tu ayuda"
English: "Thank you very much for your help"

Spanish: "¿Podrías enviarme el informe?"
English:
```

**Works well for:** Tasks with a clear pattern you want it to follow.

**Tip:** Use 2 or 3 examples. More than 5 can confuse the model and increase cost.

## Role prompting

You assign a **specific role or personality** to the AI before asking your question.

**Example:**
```
You are a lawyer specialising in intellectual property.
A client wants to know whether they can use Google images on their blog.
Explain the legal risks in simple terms.
```

**Works well for:** Getting responses with a professional perspective.

**Useful variants:**
- "You are a teacher explaining to 10-year-old children..."
- "You are a CEO giving feedback to an employee..."
- "You are a food critic reviewing a restaurant..."

## Chain-of-Thought - Basic

You ask the AI to **reason step by step** before responding.

**Example:**
```
If a train travels at 120 km/h for 2 hours and then at 80 km/h for 1 hour, what total distance does it cover? Think step by step.
```

The AI not only gives the result, but also shows the reasoning:
1. First part: 120 km/h × 2 h = 240 km
2. Second part: 80 km/h × 1 h = 80 km
3. Total: 240 + 80 = 320 km

**Works well for:** Maths problems, logic, analysis and complex decisions.

## Format prompt

You specify **how you want** the response to be structured.

**Example:**
```
Give me a healthy weekly meal plan. Use this exact format:

Day:
- Breakfast: [food]
- Lunch: [food]
- Dinner: [food]
- Snack: [food]
```

## Technique comparison

| Technique | When to use it | Example use |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| Zero-shot | Simple tasks | Classifying a sentiment |
| Few-shot | Tasks with a pattern | Translations, specific formats |
| Role | Specific perspective | Legal, medical, technical advice |
| Chain-of-Thought | Reasoning | Maths problems, analysis |
| Format | Structured output | Tables, lists, JSON |

## How to combine techniques

The most powerful thing is to **combine several techniques**:

**Combined example:**
```
You are a professional chef (role).
Give me 3 quick dinner recipes (task).
For each recipe use this format:
- Name:
- Ingredients:
- Time:
- Steps: (format)
The recipes must be vegetarian (constraint).
```
