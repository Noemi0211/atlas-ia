## What is a prompt?

A **prompt** is the instruction or message you give to an AI model to obtain a response. **Prompt engineering** is the art and science of designing these instructions to get the best possible results.

## Why it matters

The quality of what you get from AI depends directly on the quality of what you ask for:

### Bad prompt
```
Tell me about AI
```
→ Generic, superficial response with no focus

### Good prompt
```
You are an artificial intelligence expert who explains complex concepts to 15-year-old teenagers. Explain to me what AI is using a real-world analogy, in 3 short paragraphs and with a concrete example.
```
→ Specific, useful response adapted to your audience

## It is not magic, it is communication

AI cannot read your mind. It only responds exactly to what you tell it. The clearer and more structured you are, the better its response will be.

### Fundamental principle

> Garbage in, garbage out. If your prompt is vague, the response will be vague.

## Why it is a key skill

1. **Multiplies your productivity**: A good prompt achieves in seconds what a bad prompt takes minutes to achieve
2. **Separates useful results from mediocre ones**: The same AI can give you very different responses depending on how you ask
3. **Saves time**: Fewer iterations to get what you need
4. **Increases accuracy**: Reduces hallucinations and errors
5. **It is transferable**: The techniques work on most models

## Who needs to learn prompting?

- **Professionals**: To write reports, emails, analyses
- **Students**: To research, summarise, learn
- **Programmers**: To generate and debug code
- **Creators**: To generate content and designs
- **Managers**: To make informed decisions

<Callout type="tip">
You do not need to know how to program to be good at prompting. It is a communication skill, not a technical one.
</Callout>

## Common myths

### "It is just writing well"
It is not just that. It involves understanding how models think, which formats work best and how to structure complex instructions.

### "Anyone can do it"
Yes, anyone can write a prompt. But the difference between a basic prompt and an excellent one produces very different results.

### "Models will improve and it will no longer be necessary"
Although models keep improving, the ability to communicate effectively with them will always be valuable. Like knowing how to search Google: no matter how much the algorithms improve, searching well always gives better results.
