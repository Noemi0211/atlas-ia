## Using AI to Learn AI

There is something deeply elegant about using Artificial Intelligence to understand Artificial Intelligence itself. And the best part: it works incredibly well.

## Why AI is your best personal tutor

A book cannot adapt to your level. A video cannot answer your questions in real time. A teacher has time constraints.

An AI, on the other hand:

- **Adapts to your level** — You can ask it to explain as if you were 5 years old or as if you were an engineer
- **Answers your questions instantly** — No waiting, no shyness
- **Gives you personalised examples** — Based on your context, your profession, your interests
- **Never gets tired** — You can ask as many times as you need
- **Corrects you** — If your understanding is wrong, it tells you

## Prompts for learning

The difference between a generic prompt and a good one is huge. Here are real examples:

### Bad prompt

> Explain AI to me

This is too vague. The AI will give you a generic answer that is probably not what you need.

### Good prompt

> I am a secondary school teacher with no technical background. Explain what a language model (LLM) is using everyday analogies. I want to be able to explain it to my 14-year-old students.

### Excellent prompt

> I am a secondary school teacher with no technical background. Explain what a language model (LLM) is using everyday analogies. I want to be able to explain it to my 14-year-old students.
>
> I want you to:
> 1. Start with a one-line definition
> 2. Develop 3 different analogies
> 3. Give a practical example
> 4. Include the 3 most common mistakes when explaining this
> 5. Give me 3 questions my students might ask me and how to answer them

The difference is clear: **specificity + structure = better results**.

<Callout type="tip">
The best prompt for learning is one that includes: who you are, what level you have, what you want to learn, and how you want it explained to you.
</Callout>

## Study strategies with AI

### Strategy 1: The Socratic method

Ask the AI to **not give you the answer directly**, but instead to ask you questions so that you reach the conclusion yourself.

> I am learning about Machine Learning. Do not explain what overfitting is. Instead, ask me guided questions so that I discover it for myself. Only ask me one question at a time.

### Strategy 2: Explain to learn

After reading an Atlas IA lesson, ask the AI to give you an oral exam on what you have just learned.

> I have just read about Transformers. Ask me 5 questions to check that I have understood it. If I get it wrong, do not give me the right answer, but a hint.

### Strategy 3: Examples from your context

Do not settle for generic examples. Ask for examples **from your world**.

> Explain what a token is in AI. Instead of using generic examples, use examples related to teaching Mathematics in secondary school.

### Strategy 4: Compare and contrast

Ask the AI to compare concepts so that you understand the differences.

> Compare Machine Learning and Deep Learning. Use a table. Then give me 3 real situations where you would use one or the other. Be specific with real-life examples.

## Common mistakes when using AI to learn

<Callout type="error">
**Mistake 1: Confusing information with knowledge.** Reading 20 ChatGPT responses does not mean you understand something. If you cannot explain it in your own words, you do not know it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="error">
**Mistake 2: Not verifying the information.** AIs make mistakes. Always. Sometimes they invent data, references or concepts. Always verify with official sources.
</Callout>

<Callout type="error">
**Mistake 3: Using only one AI.** Each model has strengths and weaknesses. ChatGPT may be better for one thing, Claude for another. Try several.
</Callout>

<Callout type="error">
**Mistake 4: Copying without understanding.** If the AI generates code or an explanation for you, do not copy it without understanding what each part does. Ask: "Explain line by line what this does".
</Callout>

## Hands-on activity

Now it is your turn. Choose **one** of these activities:

1. **Explain it to me as if...** — Ask an AI to explain what a Transformer is as if you were 10 years old. Then explain it to a real person.

2. **The exam** — After reading this lesson, ask an AI to test you on what you have learned in the previous lessons.

3. **The mistake** — Ask an AI to explain something about AI with deliberate mistakes. Your job is to find them.

<Callout type="tip">
Practice number 3 is especially useful. If you are able to spot mistakes in AI responses, it means you really are understanding the topic.
</Callout>
