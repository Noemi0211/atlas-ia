## How AI Evolves

In 2022, ChatGPT was launched to the world and everything changed. In 2023, multimodal models appeared. In 2024, agents arrived. In 2025, AI became integrated into practically every tool we use on a daily basis.

AI does not move. **AI runs.**

## The speed of change

To put this into perspective:

- **1956**: The term "Artificial Intelligence" is coined
- **2012**: Deep Learning starts to show real results
- **2017**: The Transformers paper is published (the paper that changed everything)
- **2020**: GPT-3 demonstrates that large models work
- **2022**: ChatGPT reaches 100 million users in 2 months
- **2023**: GPT-4, Claude, Gemini — the model war
- **2024**: Agents, multimodal, AI on the desktop
- **2025**: Massive integration across all industries

Every year there is **more innovation** than in the previous decade.

## Why it matters to keep up to date

If you learned to use ChatGPT in 2023 and have not looked at it since, you are using **obsolete technology**.

Not because ChatGPT stops working, but because:

- There are now **better tools** for specific tasks
- **New capabilities** have appeared that were previously impossible
- **Best practices** have changed
- **Prices have dropped** enormously
- **Quality has risen** dramatically

<Callout type="info">
It is not about chasing every novelty. It is about understanding **trends** and knowing when something new is genuinely relevant to your use case.
</Callout>

## The waves of AI

The evolution of AI follows patterns. Identifying them will help you predict what is coming:

### Wave 1: Language models (2020-2022)

Chatbots that could hold conversations. Impressive but limited.

### Wave 2: Interactive chat (2022-2023)

ChatGPT put AI in everyone's hands. The chat interface became the standard.

### Wave 3: Multimodal (2023-2024)

AI started to understand images, audio and video. It was no longer just text.

### Wave 4: Agents (2024-2025)

AI stopped merely answering questions and started **doing things**: browsing, creating files, running code, managing pipelines.

### Wave 5: Integration (2025+)

AI is invisible. It is in your word processor, in your email, in your browser. You no longer go looking for it; it is everywhere.

## How to stay updated

### Recommended sources

- **Arxiv** — Original papers (if you have a technical background)
- **The Verge / Wired** — News in accessible language
- **Twitter/X** — Communities of researchers and professionals
- **Newsletters** — Weekly roundups of what is new

### Sources to avoid

- Influencers who promise to "revolutionize your business with AI" without explaining how
- Sensationalist content ("AI is going to replace everyone")
- Outdated tutorials more than 6 months old

<Callout type="warning">
In AI, a tutorial from 6 months ago can be completely obsolete. Always check the publication date and verify that the tool still works the same way.
</Callout>

## The beginner's paradox

Something that seems like a problem is actually an advantage:

If you start **now**, you have no bad habits to unlearn. People who have been using AI for years sometimes have habits that stop them from taking advantage of new capabilities.

You can start with best practices from day one.

## Your commitment

We are not asking you to spend hours every day reading AI news. We are asking you for something simpler:

**Every week, spend 15 minutes checking what has changed.**

A quick glance at the news. Try something new if it looks interesting. That is enough to stay relevant for years.
