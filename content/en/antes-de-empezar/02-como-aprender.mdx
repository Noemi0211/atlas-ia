## How to Learn Artificial Intelligence

Learning AI is not like learning mathematics or history. It is a field that **changes every week**, where what was true yesterday may not be true tomorrow.

That is why you need a different methodology.

## The golden rule: understand the concepts first

The most common mistake is to start using tools without understanding what lies underneath.

It is like driving a car without knowing what an engine is. It may work for a while, but when something goes wrong, you do not know what to do.

**First understand the concepts. Then use the tools.**

## How this course is structured

Each lesson follows a clear pattern:

1. **What is it?** — A simple, direct definition
2. **Why does it matter?** — Practical relevance
3. **How does it work?** — An accessible explanation
4. **When to use it?** — Real use cases
5. **When NOT to use it?** — Just as important as the above
6. **Common mistakes** — What people usually get wrong
7. **Examples** — Concrete cases
8. **Hands-on activity** — So you can check it for yourself
9. **Mini challenge** — A small challenge
10. **Self-assessment** — Measure your understanding

## Recommendations for studying

### Do not try to learn everything at once

AI is an ocean. If you try to drink it all in one gulp, you will drown. Take your time. Better one well-understood lesson than ten skimmed over.

### Take notes

It does not matter whether they are on paper, in Notion or in a Word document. **Writing down what you learn** helps consolidate knowledge. Your personal notes are your own personal map of AI.

### Try out every concept

When you learn something new, **try it immediately**. Open ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini and experiment. Practice always beats theory.

### Go back over what you do not understand

There is no shame in rereading a lesson three, five or ten times. AI concepts build on one another. If you do not understand the fundamentals, everything else wobbles.

<Callout type="tip">
A good pace is **one lesson a day**. If you have more time, spend half an hour practising what you learned. Consistency beats occasional effort.
</Callout>

## The perfect balance

Imagine a scale from 1 to 10:

- **1-3**: Pure theory (concepts, definitions)
- **4-6**: Guided practice (step-by-step examples)
- **7-10**: Free experimentation (your own projects)

Ideally, you should devote:

- **30%** to theory
- **40%** to guided practice
- **30%** to free experimentation

<Callout type="warning">
Do not fall into the trap of only reading. AI is learned **by doing**. If you have spent an hour reading about prompts, spend at least another hour writing real prompts.
</Callout>

## How to measure your progress

Do not measure your progress by how many lessons you have read. Measure it by:

- **Can you explain the concept to another person?** If you cannot, you do not understand it well
- **Can you apply it in a real case?** If you have not tried it, it is only theory
- **Can you make informed decisions?** That is the real goal
