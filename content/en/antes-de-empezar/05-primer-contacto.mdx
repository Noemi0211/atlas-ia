## Your First Contact with AI

It is time to stop reading and start doing. This lesson is a **hands-on exercise** that will guide you step by step.

## Preparation

You need access to **one of these tools** (all free):

| Tool | Link | Account required |
|-------------|--------|------------------|
| ChatGPT | chat.openai.com | Yes (free) |
| Claude | claude.ai | Yes (free) |
| Gemini | gemini.google.com | Yes (Google) |

It does not matter which one you choose. They all work for this exercise.

<Callout type="tip">
If you can, do the exercise with **all three tools**. That way you will discover from the very beginning that each AI has its own personality.
</Callout>

## Exercise 1: Introduce yourself

Open the tool you have chosen and type this:

> Hello. I am [your name], [your profession]. I am starting to learn Artificial Intelligence from scratch. Can you introduce yourself briefly and tell me what you can do to help me learn?

**Observe:**

- How does it respond to you?
- What tone does it use?
- What does it suggest you do?
- Does the answer seem useful or generic?

Write down what you observe. This will be important later.

## Exercise 2: Ask for an explanation

Now ask for this:

> Explain what Artificial Intelligence is as if you were talking to someone with no technical knowledge at all. Use an everyday analogy.

**Observe:**

- Is the analogy clear?
- Could you repeat it to someone else?
- Is there anything you did not understand?

If something is not clear to you, **ask**:

> It was not clear to me what [X] means. Can you explain it in a different way?

<Callout type="tip">
AIs learn from your conversation. The more context you give them, the better answers they give. Do not be afraid to ask "what do you mean by that?"
</Callout>

## Exercise 3: Compare responses

If you have used more than one tool, compare:

- What differences have you noticed?
- Which one seemed clearer to you?
- Which one did you like the most?

If you have only used one, now ask **another tool** for the same explanation and compare.

**This is fundamental.** From day one we want you to understand that **there is no AI that is "better" than another**. There are tools that are more suitable for each task.

## Exercise 4: Explore capabilities

Now experiment freely. Try asking it for:

1. A cooking recipe with ingredients you have at home
2. A travel plan for a city you like
3. An explanation of a topic from your professional field in a humorous tone
4. A poem about technology
5. A step-by-step solution to a maths problem

**Do not look for perfect results.** You are exploring. Observe what it does well, what it does badly, where it makes mistakes.

## Exercise 5: The hallucination test

This is the most important exercise. Ask the AI:

> Who won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2024?

If the AI gives you a name without hesitation, **do not trust it 100%**. Verify the answer with a Google search.

<Callout type="warning">
AIs can invent information with total confidence. This is called **hallucination**. Always verify important data with official sources. You will learn more about this in Block 1.
</Callout>

## What you have just done

Without realising it, in these 5 exercises you have:

- **Interacted** with an AI model
- **Evaluated** the quality of its responses
- **Compared** different tools
- **Detected** a limitation (hallucinations)
- **Started** to develop your own judgment

That is exactly what we are going to build throughout this whole course: **judgment**.

<Callout type="tip">
Save screenshots of today's interactions. In a few months you will look at them and be amazed at how far you have come.
</Callout>
