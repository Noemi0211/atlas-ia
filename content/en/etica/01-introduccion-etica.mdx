## Why ethics matters

Artificial intelligence is not neutral. Every model, every piece of training data and every implementation decision reflects human values, priorities and biases. As AI becomes integrated into every aspect of society, ethical questions are no longer optional: they are urgent.

<Callout type="info">
AI ethics is not an obstacle to innovation: it is the foundation on which innovation that benefits the whole of society must be built.
</Callout>

## Fundamental principles

### Transparency

AI systems must be understandable and explainable. Users have the right to know when they are interacting with an AI and how the decisions that affect them are made.

### Fairness

AI must treat all people equitably, without discriminating on the basis of gender, race, age, origin or any other protected characteristic.

### Accountability

Those who develop and deploy AI systems must take responsibility for their impacts. Blame cannot be delegated to an algorithm.

### Privacy

Personal data must be protected. AI must not collect, use or share information without informed consent.

### Social benefit

AI must be designed to benefit humanity, not just to maximise economic profits.

<Callout type="tip">
These five principles (transparency, fairness, accountability, privacy and social benefit) are the foundation of most AI ethics frameworks in the world.
</Callout>

## Current ethical dilemmas

### Who is responsible when an AI makes a mistake?

If a self-driving vehicle causes an accident, is the manufacturer, the software developer, the owner or the AI itself responsible? There is still no clear legal answer.

### Should we allow AI in critical decisions?

AI systems already take part in decisions about hiring, loans, medical diagnoses and court sentences. To what extent should we delegate these decisions to algorithms?

### What about displaced jobs?

AI-driven automation is transforming the labour market. Who is responsible for the reskilling and protection of workers?

## Reference ethics frameworks

| Organisation | Framework | Key principles |
|-------------|-----------|----------------|
| UNESCO | Recommendation on AI | Proportionality, transparency, human oversight |
| European Union | AI Act | Risk, transparency, fundamental rights |
| OECD | AI Principles | Inclusiveness, sustainable growth, transparency |
| IEEE | Ethically Aligned Design | Human rights, wellbeing, accountability |

## The cost of ignoring ethics

The cases of unethical AI are not hypothetical:

- **Bias in hiring:** recruitment systems that discriminated against women
- **Facial recognition:** much higher error rates for people of colour
- **Social networks:** algorithms that amplify disinformation and polarisation
- **Credit:** models that denied loans based on postal code
