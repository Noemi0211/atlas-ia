## Block summary

In this block we have explored the ethical, legal and social dimensions of artificial intelligence.

### Lesson 1 — Introduction to ethics
AI is not neutral. Five fundamental principles guide AI ethics: transparency, fairness, accountability, privacy and social benefit. Ignoring ethics has real, documented consequences.

### Lesson 2 — Privacy and data
The GDPR establishes data protection principles that AI challenges: informed consent, minimisation, the right to be forgotten. Techniques such as differential privacy seek to reconcile AI and privacy.

### Lesson 3 — Bias and fairness
Algorithmic bias originates in the data, the model or the deployment. Real cases (facial recognition, hiring, criminal justice) demonstrate its consequences. Mitigation requires action at all stages.

### Lesson 4 — Copyright and intellectual property
Generative AI has broken the traditional copyright frameworks. The debate over the use of protected data for training is in the courts. Transparency and attribution are essential good practices.

### Lesson 5 — Regulation and the future
The European AI Act leads risk-based regulation. The US, China and Latin America have different approaches. The challenges include the speed of technological change, the global nature of AI and the balance between innovation and protection.

## Final self-assessment

1. What are the five ethical principles of AI?
2. What rights does the GDPR grant to citizens over their data?
3. How is bias introduced into AI systems?
4. Can an AI be the author of a copyright-protected work?
5. What is the difference between minimal, limited, high and unacceptable risk in the AI Act?
6. What challenges does regulating a technology that evolves so quickly raise?
7. What is your responsibility as a user or creator of AI?

## Block glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Algorithmic bias** | Systematic prejudices reflected in an algorithm's decisions |
| **GDPR** | General Data Protection Regulation (European legislation) |
| **Differential privacy** | Technique that adds noise to protect individual data |
| **Fair use** | Legal doctrine that allows limited use of protected works |
| **AI Act** | European AI regulation based on risk level |
| **XAI** | Explainable AI: systems that can explain their decisions |
| **Algorithmic audit** | Independent evaluation of AI systems |

## Next steps

- **Review:** audit the AI tools you use regularly. What data do they collect? How do they use it?
- **Debate:** organise a conversation about AI ethics with your class group or your students
- **Stay informed:** follow the evolution of the AI Act and the regulations in your country
- **Act:** demand transparency from AI companies and practise responsible use
