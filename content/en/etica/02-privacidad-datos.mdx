## Data as a critical resource

AI feeds on data. The more data a model has, the better it performs. But this hunger for data clashes head-on with the right to privacy. Managing this tension is one of the most important ethical and legal challenges of our time.

<Callout type="info">
Every interaction with an AI generates data. Every prompt, every uploaded image, every conversation. The question is not whether your data is being used, but how, by whom and for what purposes.
</Callout>

## General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

### Fundamental principles

The European GDPR establishes the foundations of data protection in the digital age:

1. **Lawfulness, fairness and transparency:** data must be processed in a legal and honest way
2. **Purpose limitation:** only for specific and explicit purposes
3. **Data minimisation:** only the necessary data
4. **Accuracy:** correct and up-to-date data
5. **Storage limitation:** kept only for the necessary time
6. **Integrity and confidentiality:** secure against unauthorised access

### User rights

- **Access:** knowing what data companies hold
- **Rectification:** correcting inaccurate data
- **Erasure (right to be forgotten):** deleting personal data
- **Objection:** refusing the processing of their data
- **Portability:** taking their data to another service

## Specific challenges of AI

### Informed consent

Can a user give informed consent if they do not understand how their data will be used to train models? The opacity of AI systems makes it difficult to meet the transparency requirement.

### Derived data

AI can infer sensitive information from seemingly innocuous data. For example, by analysing browsing history it is possible to infer political orientation, religion or health status.

### Training and forgetting

The right to be forgotten clashes with the nature of trained models: once a piece of data is used for training, it cannot be selectively removed from the model without retraining it completely.

<Callout type="warning">
Many free AI tools use your conversations to train their models. If you share sensitive or confidential information, it could become incorporated into future responses to other users.
</Callout>

## Protection strategies

### For users

- Do not share sensitive personal information with public AIs
- Review and adjust privacy settings
- Use anonymous or temporary modes when possible
- Read privacy policies before using tools

### For organisations

- Carry out data protection impact assessments
- Implement privacy by design
- Anonymise and pseudonymise data before using it with AI
- Establish clear retention and deletion policies
- Train staff in privacy and security

### For developers

- Minimise data collection from the design stage
- Offer clear opt-out options
- Implement end-to-end encryption
- Document which data is used and for what

## Differential privacy

A promising technique that makes it possible to train models without compromising individual data. It adds controlled statistical noise so that specific individuals cannot be identified in the results, while maintaining the overall usefulness of the data.

<Callout type="tip">
Apple and Google already use differential privacy to improve their services without accessing specific personal data. It is one of the most promising techniques for reconciling AI and privacy.
</Callout>
