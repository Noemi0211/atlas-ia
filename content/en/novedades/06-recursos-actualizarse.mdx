## The challenge of keeping up

Artificial intelligence is probably the fastest-evolving field in the history of technology. What is new today is standard tomorrow and obsolete the day after. Staying up to date is not optional: it is part of working with AI.

<Callout type="info">
In AI, knowledge has an expiry date. Accepting that you will always be out of date on something is the first step to staying up to date on what matters.
</Callout>

## Information sources

### Academic publications

- **arXiv:** main repository of AI scientific papers. Search by category: cs.CL (language), cs.CV (vision), cs.LG (learning)
- **Papers with Code:** papers with associated code and benchmark results
- **Google Scholar:** tracking of authors and citations
- **Semantic Scholar:** semantic search of papers with AI

### Blogs and newsletters

- **The Batch (Andrew Ng):** weekly summary of the most important news
- **Import AI (Jack Clark):** deep analysis of the ecosystem
- **TLDR AI:** concise daily summary
- **Latent Space:** podcast and newsletter on applied AI
- **Sequoia AI Ascent:** perspective from investment

### Communities

- **Hugging Face Hub:** community + repository of models and datasets
- **Reddit:** r/MachineLearning, r/LocalLLaMA, r/StableDiffusion
- **Discord:** servers for specific tools and models
- **X/Twitter:** follow key researchers and professionals

### Technical documentation

- **Model Context Protocol (MCP):** the standard for connecting agents with tools; its official documentation is the best gateway
- **OpenAI, Anthropic and Google docs:** each company publishes guides and news about their models and APIs
- **GitHub:** follow the repositories of protocols (MCP, A2A) and frameworks (LangChain, CrewAI)

<Callout type="tip">
Don't try to read everything. Select 2-3 high-quality sources and establish a routine. It is better to read 10 minutes a day than 2 hours once a month.
</Callout>

## Continuous learning strategies

### The 80/20 rule

80% of the value lies in 20% of the information. Focus on:

- General trends, not specific details
- Fundamental concepts that do not change
- Practical tools you can use today
- Most cited papers and publications

### Project-based learning

The best way to learn AI is to build things:

1. Identify a problem you are interested in
2. Look for tools and models that can solve it
3. Implement a solution, even a simple one
4. Share it and receive feedback
5. Repeat with more complex problems

### Maintaining a personal archive

Create your own knowledge system:

- Bookmark important papers (with a note on why)
- Save effective prompts
- Document experiments and results
- Keep a list of tested tools

## Learning schedule

### Daily (5-10 min)

- Read TLDR AI or a news summary
- Check new models on Hugging Face
- Follow the news of protocols such as MCP
- One interaction with ChatGPT or Claude on a new topic

### Weekly (30-60 min)

- Read The Batch or Import AI
- Explore 2-3 new relevant papers
- Try a new tool or model
- Write a summary of what you learned

### Monthly (2-3 hours)

- Do a small project with new technologies
- Read a deep analysis of a trend
- Take part in a community or event
- Review and update your personal archive
