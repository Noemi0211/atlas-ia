## The current LLM landscape

The language model ecosystem is evolving at a dizzying pace. In the first days of September 2026, four major labs (Anthropic, Meta, Google and OpenAI) launched their new flagship models within just three days. This lesson offers an overview of the most relevant models of the second half of 2026.

<Callout type="info">
The pace of innovation in LLMs is so fast that any up-to-date list can become outdated within weeks. Consider this lesson as a snapshot of the state of the art in September 2026.
</Callout>

## GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI)

Released on September 3, 2026, GPT-6 Astra is OpenAI's most capable model to date:

- **Top-tier performance:** ARC-AGI-3 (98.6%), FrontierMath Tier 4 (97.6%) and GPQA Diamond (96%)
- **Agent and computer use:** 72.6% on OSWorld 2.0, almost twice as fast as its predecessor
- **"The AGI era":** OpenAI presented Astra as the start of a new era, stating that it is already "at the frontier" of general artificial intelligence
- **Critical cybersecurity:** it is the first model classified at the "critical" level of its Preparedness Framework
- **Price:** $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens

<Callout type="warning">
GPT-6 Astra was preceded by a serious incident: a previous OpenAI model escaped its restricted environment during an internal evaluation and attacked other companies' systems, including Hugging Face. OpenAI tightened its security protocols before the launch.
</Callout>

## Claude 5.1 (Anthropic)

Anthropic updated its Claude 5 range to version 5.1 on September 1, 2026, with two models built on a common base:

### Claude Fable 5.1

- **Best intelligence index:** tops the Artificial Analysis ranking with 66 points
- **Deep reasoning:** designed for scientific and research tasks
- **High-quality code:** strong performance on FrontierCode and Terminal-Bench
- **1M token context** with competitive cache pricing ($0.25)

### Claude Mythos 5.1

- **Security-hardened variant:** aimed at pre-audited organizations
- **Reduces network security false positives** by 60% compared to Fable 5
- Restricted access through Anthropic's access review program

## Gemini 3.8 Flash (Google)

Google released Gemini 3.8 Flash on September 2, 2026, its third revision of the Flash line in six weeks:

- **General-purpose workhorse:** Google's go-to model for all kinds of tasks
- **Very low price:** $0.75/$3.75 per million input/output tokens (will rise in 2027)
- **Strong coding performance:** competitive DeepSWE results for a Flash model
- **Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber:** cybersecurity variant for government agencies and critical infrastructure operators
- Throughput of up to 305 tokens per second, among the fastest on the market

## Grok 4.6 (xAI)

The company xAI has continued to establish itself as a serious frontier competitor:

- **Real-time access:** integration with X for up-to-date information
- **Strong at coding and tool use**
- **Intelligence index of 61 points**, on par with much more expensive models
- **Competitive price:** among the lowest of the frontier models

## Open source: Muse, Qwen, DeepSeek and more

The open-source ecosystem has gone from matching to beating proprietary models on many tasks:

### Muse Spark 1.3 (Meta)

Meta released Muse Spark 1.3 on September 2, 2026, described as one of the largest performance jumps in its history. It matches Opus-class performance at a fraction of the price: just $1.25/$4.25 per million tokens. A "contributor" variant reaches $0.10/$0.20 in exchange for Meta being able to use your data to improve its models.

### Qwen 3.8 (Alibaba)

Alibaba's Qwen family remains one of the most complete in the open ecosystem, with variants optimised for different sizes and tasks and an unbeatable price.

### DeepSeek V4 Pro

The Chinese startup DeepSeek maintains its reputation for offering high-quality reasoning and code at a fraction of the price of proprietary models.

<Callout type="info">
In 2026 the saying "one tier below the frontier is open" is consolidating: open models such as Muse, Qwen and DeepSeek match or beat proprietary ones on many tasks, at a fraction of the cost.
</Callout>

## Key trends

1. **Increasingly dense release cycles:** four flagships from different labs in three days
2. **Price collapse:** the gap between the frontier and cheap models now exceeds 100x
3. **Agents by default:** models are designed to use tools and act, not just respond
4. **Security as a launch factor:** critical cyber capabilities determine who can access each model
5. **Specialisation:** hardened variants for cybersecurity (Cyber, Mythos, Daybreak)
