## Deep Learning and neural networks

If Machine Learning is learning from data, Deep Learning is learning from data **with layers and layers of abstraction**.

It is like the difference between learning to add and learning calculus. Both are mathematics, but one has much more depth than the other.

## What is an artificial neural network?

A neural network is a system inspired by the human brain. It is made up of **artificial neurons** organized in layers.

### The teamwork analogy

Imagine a company with three departments:

1. **Input department** (input layer): Receives the raw information — an image, a text, some data
2. **Intermediate departments** (hidden layers): Process the information, each at its own level of abstraction
3. **Output department** (output layer): Produces the result — "this is a cat", "this email is spam"

In a computer image, for example:

- The first layer detects **edges** and colors
- The second layer combines edges to detect **shapes**
- The third layer combines shapes to detect **objects** (eyes, ears)
- The final layer recognizes the **whole animal** (cat, dog)

## Why "deep"?

Because it has **many intermediate layers**. A network with few layers might have 3-5 layers. A "deep" network can have **tens or hundreds of layers**.

Each layer learns **more complex** representations of the data.

```
Entrada → [Capa 1: bordes] → [Capa 2: formas] → [Capa 3: objetos] → Salida
```

<Callout type="info">
Deep Learning works especially well when there is **a lot of data** and the problem requires understanding **hierarchical** patterns — such as in images, text, audio or video.
</Callout>

## How training works

### Forward pass

The data enters through the initial layer and passes through each layer until it produces an output. Each neuron applies a simple mathematical operation.

### Error calculation

The model's output is compared with the correct answer. The difference is the **error**.

### Backpropagation

Here is the magic. The error is sent **backwards** through the network, adjusting each neuron's parameters to reduce the error.

### Iteration

This process is repeated **millions of times** with thousands of examples. Gradually, the network improves.

## Important types of neural networks

### Convolutional neural networks (CNN)

Specialized in **images**. They detect visual patterns such as edges, textures and objects.

**Use**: Facial recognition, medical imaging diagnosis, autonomous driving.

### Recurrent neural networks (RNN/LSTM)

Designed for **sequences** — text, audio, time series. They process information in order.

**Use**: Translation, text prediction, sentiment analysis.

### Transformers

The dominant architecture today. They process **the whole sequence at once** instead of one by one. This makes them much faster and more powerful.

**Use**: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — all the big language models.

### Generative adversarial networks (GAN)

Two networks compete against each other: one generates fake content and the other tries to detect whether it is fake. The result is increasingly realistic content.

**Use**: Image generation, deepfakes, super-resolution.

## Deep Learning in real life

| Application | Network used | Impact |
|------------|--------------|---------|
| ChatGPT/Claude | Transformer | Conversation and text generation |
| DALL-E/Midjourney | Diffusion + Transformer | Image generation |
| Facial recognition | CNN | Security, phone unlocking |
| Autonomous car | CNN + Transformer | Assisted driving |
| Medical diagnosis | CNN | Early disease detection |
| Translation | Transformer | High-quality automatic translation |

## Limitations of Deep Learning

Not everything is perfect:

- **It needs enormous amounts of data**: It works well with millions of examples
- **It is computationally expensive**: Training a large model can cost millions of dollars
- **It is a black box**: Sometimes we do not know why it makes certain decisions
- **It is prone to overfitting**: It can memorize instead of learn

<Callout type="warning">
Deep Learning is not the solution to everything. For many problems, a simple model (such as a regression or a decision tree) works just as well or better. Choosing the right tool is key.
</Callout>
