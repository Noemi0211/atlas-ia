## Transformers: the revolution

If you had to point to **a single moment** that changed modern AI, it would be the publication of the paper "Attention Is All You Need" in 2017.

That paper introduced **Transformers**, and nothing has been the same since.

## Before Transformers

Language models used **recurrent networks (RNN/LSTM)**. They processed text **word by word**, in sequence.

### The problem

- **Slowness**: They could not process text in parallel
- **Short memory**: They forgot information from the first words by the time they reached the last ones
- **Limited scalability**: They could not grow much

It was like reading a book word by word without being able to look back.

## The revolution: the attention mechanism

Transformers do not process text in sequence. They process **the whole sequence at once**.

### The translator analogy

Imagine you translate a sentence from Spanish to English:

> "El gato se sentó en la alfombra"

A human translator does not translate word by word. They look at **the whole sentence** and understand the relationships:

- "El" refers to "gato"
- "se sentó" is the action
- "en la alfombra" is the location

The **attention** mechanism does exactly this: it calculates the **relevance** of each word in relation to all the other words in the sequence.

## How the Transformer works

### Encoder

It processes the input and creates an understandable representation of the text. Each word is represented as a vector that captures its meaning in the context of the sentence.

### Decoder

It generates the output, word by word, using the representation created by the encoder.

### Multi-Head Attention

Instead of looking at the relationships from a single angle, the Transformer looks **from multiple angles simultaneously**. It is as if several expert translators analyzed the same sentence from different perspectives.

### Positional Encoding

Since the Transformer processes everything at once (not in order), it needs a way to know **the order of the words**. Positional encoding adds information about the position of each word.

## Why Transformers won

| Feature | RNN/LSTM | Transformer |
|---------------|----------|------------|
| Processing | Sequential | Parallel |
| Memory | Short | Long (context window) |
| Scalability | Limited | Enormous |
| Training speed | Slow | Fast |
| Quality | Good | Excellent |

<Callout type="info">
Transformers can process full texts at once because they use a mechanism called "self-attention" that calculates all the relationships simultaneously. This makes it possible to train enormous models in parallel using GPUs.
</Callout>

## Transformer variants

### Encoder-only (BERT)

It only encodes. Excellent for **understanding** text — classification, sentiment analysis, information extraction.

### Decoder-only (GPT)

It only decodes. Excellent for **generating** text — chatbots, content generation, code.

### Encoder-Decoder (T5, BART)

Both. Excellent for **transforming** text — translation, summarization, rewriting.

## Transformers today

Today, **practically all** the important language models are Transformers:

- GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI)
- Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic)
- Gemini 3.8 (Google)
- Muse Spark 1.3 (Meta)
- Qwen 3.8 (Alibaba)
- BERT (Google, for search)

Not only in language. Also in:

- **Computer vision** (ViT — Vision Transformer)
- **Audio** (Whisper for transcription)
- **Multimodal** (models that understand text + image + audio)

</Callout>
