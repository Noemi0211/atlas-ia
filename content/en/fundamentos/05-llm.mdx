## Large Language Models (LLM)

LLMs are the models behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and almost all the generative AI we use today.

They are, possibly, the most impactful technology of the last decade.

## What is an LLM?

A Large Language Model is a Machine Learning model trained on **enormous amounts of text** to learn patterns of human language.

It can:

- Generate coherent text
- Answer questions
- Translate languages
- Summarize documents
- Write code
- Hold conversations

All without being specifically programmed for each task. It is a **generalist** model of language.

## The voracious reader analogy

Imagine someone who has read **everything ever written on the internet** — all the books, articles, code, conversations, everything.

That person would not be an expert in any one thing, but would have an **incredibly broad** understanding of language and human knowledge.

That is an LLM. It has "read" an immense amount of text and has learned:

- How sentences are structured
- Which words go together
- How to respond to different types of questions
- What tone to use in different contexts

## How they are trained

### Step 1: Data collection

**Billions of tokens** of text are collected from the internet, books, scientific articles, code, etc.

### Step 2: Predictive training

The model is trained to **predict the next word**. Given a text, what is the most likely word that comes next?

> "The sky is..." → "blue" (with high probability)

This process is repeated **billions of times** with different texts.

### Step 3: Fine-tuning

After general training, the model is tuned to follow instructions and be useful.

### Step 4: RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)

Humans evaluate the model's responses and give it feedback on what is good and what is not. The model learns to produce more useful and safer responses.

<Callout type="info">
The RLHF process is what makes ChatGPT so useful. Without it, the model would generate correct text but not necessarily useful or safe text.
</Callout>

## Parameters: size matters

LLMs are measured by their number of **parameters**. Parameters are the "internal switches" the model adjusts during training.

| Model | Year | Parameters |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| GPT-2 | 2019 | 1.5 billion |
| GPT-3 | 2020 | 175 billion |
| GPT-4 | 2023 | ~1.8 trillion (estimated) |
| Claude 3.5 | 2024 | Not disclosed |
| Gemini Ultra | 2024 | ~540 billion |
| GPT-6 Astra | 2026 | Not disclosed |

The more parameters, generally the more capability, but also the more computational cost.

## Emergent capabilities

Something fascinating happens when models reach a certain size: they develop **capabilities for which they were not explicitly trained**.

- Logical reasoning
- Solving mathematical problems
- Coding
- Understanding metaphors
- Reasoning about the real world

These capabilities **"emerge"** from the size and diversity of the training data.

## Limitations of LLMs

### Limited memory

LLMs do not really "remember" previous conversations. The context window limits how much text they can process at once.

### No real-time internet access

A pure LLM does not know what is happening right now. It only knows what was in its training data.

### They can be wrong

LLMs **invent data** with total confidence. This is called hallucination and it is one of the most important limitations.

### They do not reason like humans

Although it seems like they reason, they are actually **predicting statistical patterns**. They have no real understanding of the world.

<Callout type="warning">
Never blindly trust an LLM with critical data. Always verify important information with primary sources.
</Callout>
